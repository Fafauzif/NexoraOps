Refine the existing Nexora interface using the attached JakCool and Lintara screenshots as the primary visual references.

The current design feels cramped, fragmented, and overloaded. Redesign its layout and visual hierarchy substantially. Preserve the logistics functionality, but distribute information more thoughtfully across pages, tabs, and contextual panels.

DESIGN DIRECTION

Create a spacious, light, calm operations platform with readable typography, large analytical visuals, and clear priorities.

Follow JakCool for the application shell, sidebar, page headings, chart layouts, and supporting panels. Follow Lintara for the immersive digital twin, compact metrics strip, and timeline controls. Adapt these patterns to Nexora’s logistics domain without copying their branding or station-specific content.

LAYOUT AND DENSITY

Design at a realistic desktop viewport of 1440 × 900. Keep text readable at normal zoom.

Use a collapsible sidebar around 224–240 px wide, a restrained top bar, and generous content margins.

Give each page one dominant visual: a map, 3D twin, forecast chart, heatmap, or financial comparison.

Use a maximum of two main content columns. Supporting panels should remain clearly secondary.

Show only three or four essential headline metrics.

Use 24–32 px spacing between major sections and comfortable padding within panels.

Allow vertical scrolling. Do not shrink content to fit every detail into the first viewport.

Reveal detailed information through selection, tabs, expandable sections, and drawers.

Avoid nested cards, excessive borders, permanent warning boxes, and rows of tiny widgets.

TYPOGRAPHY AND COLOUR

Use a clean sans-serif with approximately 16 px body text, 14 px supporting labels, and 28–32 px page titles. Avoid tiny uppercase technical labels and excessive monospace text.

Use a very light grey background, white surfaces, dark slate text, subtle borders, and a restrained teal or blue accent. Reserve orange and red for actionable warnings. Use colour consistently across maps and charts.

NAVIGATION

Support these connected pages:
Overview; Digital Twin; Forecasts; Policy Lab; Capacity & Partners; Incidents & Actions; Impact; Rollout.

Place Evidence & Assumptions in a secondary navigation position. Group the main navigation under Monitor, Decide, and Evaluate.

PAGE COMPOSITION

Digital Twin:
Make the geographic network or 3D scene occupy most of the workspace. Place a slim metrics strip above it and playback/timeline controls below. Open hub or lane details when an object is selected. Keep detailed scenario settings behind a clearly labelled control.

Forecasts:
Use a large chart with a clear forecast boundary and uncertainty band. Add a smaller supporting panel explaining demand drivers and capacity risks. Put secondary charts further down the page. Provide simple time-horizon controls.

Policy Lab:
Give the heatmap enough room to read comfortably. Use colour to communicate the overall pattern, with detailed values shown on hover or selection. Keep only essential controls visible. Selecting a policy reveals its outcome distribution, service trade-offs, and an “Apply policy” action.

Incidents & Actions:
Use a readable incident list and a focused selected-incident workspace. Present the problem in plain language, then compare response options with clear cost, capacity, and service consequences.

Impact:
Start with a few prominent outcomes. Follow with a large operating-profit bridge and baseline-versus-proposed comparison. Place investment returns, cash flows, assumptions, and detailed tables below or in tabs.

INTERACTION AND COPY

Keep the selected scenario, geography, and comparison baseline consistent across pages. Use plain labels such as “Delivery cost per parcel,” “Capacity shortfall,” and “Compare responses.”

Include a discreet “Prototype · simulated data” indicator. Treat all example numbers as illustrative. Do not invent live integrations, measured forecast accuracy, or validated savings.

Generate the Digital Twin, Forecasts, Policy Lab, and Impact screens first, using one consistent design system. Prioritise a clear first glance: the user should immediately understand the page’s purpose, main result, and next action.

Judge the result by readability and hierarchy, not by how much information fits on screen.

# Product brief

## Intended experience

NexoraOps presents the proposed platform as an operating logistics product. A judge can inspect the network, change a decision, compare consequences, respond to disruption, and explain the investment result without reading implementation notes.

The product connects three practical questions: where to allocate parcels, how to preserve service when capacity changes, and whether the investment improves the group financial result. The default scenario is Greater Jakarta, and the default view is a navigable 3D regional twin on desktop.

## Shared state

Policy family, maximum internal ceiling, planning utilization limit, workload multiplier, available capacity, travel condition, cost inflation, discount rate, and non-logistics contribution carry across analytical views. The regional incident is a separately scoped four-hour event: applying it selects the incident's defined base policy, resets operating stress controls, and updates regional allocations. It does not silently annualize a single disruption into five-year savings.

The timeline controls the illustrative replay. Daily and annual metrics retain their full-period scope. Moving an actor represents aggregate activity; it does not represent a GPS-tracked vehicle or one physical parcel.

## Presentation language

Use direct operating language: “Jaringan pengiriman,” “Bandingkan respons,” “Terapkan skenario,” and “Dampak keuangan.” Avoid assistant commentary, work-in-progress badges, generic sales filler, and review residue. One discreet prototype/data indicator is sufficient. Relevant assumptions belong beside the affected metric or in evidence details.

Financial results remain exact even when unfavorable. A presentation-ready product can show that a policy has a positive project NPV while the wider group-profit constraint does not pass. It cannot describe an unpassed constraint as a successful outcome.

## Boundaries

This deliverable is an offline presentation prototype with functional local calculations. It provides no live company connection, account system, operational dispatch integration, production authorization, trained forecasting service, or street-level geospatial inventory. These technical boundaries appear in handoff documentation, rather than as repeated drafting warnings in the interface.

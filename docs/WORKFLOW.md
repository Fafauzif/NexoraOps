# Presentation and review requirements

These user instructions apply to NexoraOps and future work on its deliverables.

## Audience and voice

Present the final idea as an operating product: “If NexoraOps already existed, here is what it would look like.” Judges understand that it is a prototype. Do not fill the product with “Need validation,” “Further justification needed,” “Planned,” or equivalent drafting status. Keep material definitions and assumptions where they help the reader interpret the result. Do not imply live telemetry, measured savings, research findings, investment approval, or empirical forecast accuracy that does not exist.

Create a finished, presentation-ready deliverable for its intended audience. Adapt the language, tone, and structure to the task—business, academic, technical, or creative. Write naturally and precisely, with specific content rather than generic filler, inflated claims, or repetitive summaries.

Keep assistant-to-user commentary out of the file. Do not include conversational introductions, explanations of what was created, references to prompts or AI tools, offers of further help, or instructions addressed conversationally to the requester. Avoid “key takeaway” boxes, canned conclusions, decorative callouts, and unnecessary explanations. Include substantive analysis, methodology, definitions, and limitations when the reader needs them.

Use clean, restrained design: consistent typography, clear hierarchy, sensible spacing, aligned tables, and a limited palette suited to the project. Avoid checkerboard patterns, automatic alternating row fills, excessive borders, and decoration without a purpose. Follow supplied templates and brand guidelines, subject to explicit user preferences.

For XLSX, do not freeze rows or columns. Use readable dimensions, consistent units and number formats, functional formulas where appropriate, and purposeful formatting for inputs, headings and totals. For CSV, preserve a clean, consistent data structure without decorative rows or commentary. For DOCX and PDF, use coherent headings, readable tables, and deliberate pagination without clipped content or awkward breaks.

Use genuine, traceable sources. Cite external evidence appropriately and provide APA 7 references where applicable, using real bibliographic details and links or DOIs. Do not expose upload filenames, local paths, tool identifiers, or internal cell references as audience-facing citations. Identify supplied material by a meaningful document title or accurate description. Label estimates “Planning assumptions” or “Team assumptions,” as appropriate. Never invent attribution, research or approval.

Ask before material new assumptions affecting scope, conclusions, financial results or interpretation. Resolve routine wording and formatting independently. Current confirmed scope and recorded planning assumptions already have authorization; do not re-open them as unnecessary permission requests.

Before delivery, check calculations, citations, consistency and layout, and remove drafting residue. Keep the handoff brief and put necessary production notes outside audience-facing files.

## Review-and-revision method

1. Define success using required content, constraints, audience, references and standards. Resolve routine choices independently; clarify material new assumptions.
2. Produce a complete first attempt.
3. Perform a distinct demanding review. Use an independent critic when available; claim independent review only when it occurred. Compare the actual output with requirements and references.
4. Score applicable dimensions from 0–10: task fulfillment, correctness, completeness, clarity/usability, and presentation/execution. A 5 needs substantial revision, 7 is usable with clear weaknesses, 8.5 is strong with minor issues, and 9 needs exceptional supporting evidence. A 10 is extraordinary across every dimension. Justify judgments with observable strengths and defects.
5. Verify calculations, formulas, sources, behavior, layout and consistency where possible. Inspect actual output. Distinguish tested results from subjective judgments and unchecked claims.
6. Rank issues by impact. Correct critical errors and unmet requirements, then substantive weaknesses, then polish. Recheck affected behavior after revision.

Repeat for up to four substantive rounds. Stop when applicable dimensions reach at least 8.5 and no critical errors or explicit unmet requirements remain. Do not increase scores merely to pass, and do not continue without a concrete improvement. Deliver the strongest verified result, disclosing material unresolved limitations outside the audience-facing deliverable. Do not claim perfection or checks that did not occur. Keep ratings and critique exchanges out of deliverables unless requested.
